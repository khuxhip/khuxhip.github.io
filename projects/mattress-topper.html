Intelligent Mattress Topper

October 2024 – August 2025

Add a photo of the build here

A sensor-integrated mattress pad designed to monitor pressure distribution and help caregivers identify areas at risk of developing pressure injuries.

Project Overview

Pressure injuries affect patients who remain in one position for extended periods and can lead to serious complications, longer hospital stays, and increased demands on healthcare staff. Existing prevention methods often depend on caregivers manually repositioning patients at regular intervals, which can be difficult to maintain in understaffed care environments. Our team developed a mattress pad that continuously measures pressure across a patient’s body and converts the sensor data into an easy-to-read pressure map. The system is designed in mind to help caregivers recognize high-pressure areas early and make more informed decisions about when a patient should be adjusted.

Aim

The goal of the project was to create a comfortable and noninvasive system that could continuously monitor pressure without disrupting the patient. By presenting pressure data in a visual format, the system aimed to help caregivers identify prolonged pressure exposure, respond more quickly, and reduce the likelihood of pressure ulcers.

System Design

The prototype consists of a flexible textile layer containing embedded pressure sensors placed beneath a medical-grade foam pad. An Arduino microcontroller collects the readings from the sensor array, while a multiplexer and analog-to-digital conversion system allows multiple sensor inputs to be processed. The collected data is then sent to a software interface that displays a color-coded pressure map. Areas experiencing greater pressure appear more prominently, allowing users to quickly understand how weight is distributed across the surface of the mattress.

Design Process

The project began by researching pressure injury formation, existing prevention methods, and the needs of patients and healthcare workers. This helped define the system requirements, including comfort, continuous monitoring, sensor sensitivity, etc. We then developed and tested several prototypes. Due to the sensors being embedded within a soft surface, we had to balance measurement accuracy with patient comfort and durability. Testing required us to examine sensor placement, wiring, calibration, and how the foam layers affected the pressure readings. As the project progressed, we narrowed down our goal from creating a fully automated pressure-relief system to first developing a reliable platform. This allowed us to focus on building a strong foundation that future teams could expand into an active intervention system.

Results

The final prototype successfully detected and quantified differences in pressure across the mattress pad and displayed the readings through a real-time visual pressure map. This demonstrated the system’s ability to identify concentrated pressure areas and monitor how pressure distribution changed over time. Although automated patient repositioning could not be achieved at the time of development due to deadlines, the project established the sensing, data collection, and visualization foundation needed for a future adaptive pressure-relief system. The project was presented at the 2025 Engineering Open House under American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME), MediMech, & Carle Institute of Health, where it received second place in the Most Industry Impact category presented by Chevron. This project is now in further developments under a different team, who recently won first place at the 2026 Engineering Open House for Most Industry Impact, and was presented at COZAD at UIUC.
